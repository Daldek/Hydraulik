## 1. University of Sheffield physical model of sewer–surface interaction (Rubinato, Martins, Shucksmith, Kesserwani, Djordjević, Leandro): citations, measurements, data availability, models validated

### Takeaway
The Sheffield facility is a 1:6-scale linked sewer–floodplain model with an 8.2 m × 4 m acrylic floodplain (slope 0.001), a 75 mm sewer pipe and a 240 mm manhole. It is the best-documented laboratory dataset for manhole exchange flows (sewer→surface surcharge and surface→sewer drainage, steady and unsteady). It was produced by the EPSRC "Flood Interact" project (ended June 2018), and some of its data are openly deposited on Zenodo. The data were used to validate HLLC/Roe finite-volume SWE models, a 2D finite-difference model, quasi-steady and dynamic manhole-exchange models (Liège), a coupled ODE–SWE solver and CFD models. I found no evidence that SWMM-based couplings (Iber-SWMM, Itzï) or SIPSON were validated against it. One correction to the brief: I found **no "Rubinato et al. 2018, Journal of Hydraulic Research"**. The 2018 papers are in *J. Hydroinformatics* and *Urban Water J.*; the only *J. Hydraul. Res.* paper found is Muraro et al. (2025), about contaminant transport.

### Inferences
- Model groups validated against Sheffield data so far: Riemann-solver FV SWE (HLLC/Roe), a 2D finite-difference model (JHydroinf 2018), quasi-steady and dynamic manhole-exchange models (Liège/Sheffield 2021), a coupled ODE–SWE network solver (2024) and 3D CFD (2020). No SWMM-based coupling appears among them, so a SFINCS–SWMM validation on this dataset would be novel.
- Most published uses focus on the **exchange formula** (weir/orifice coefficients, head definition), not on full 2D flood fields. The most useful quantities for SFINCS–SWMM are therefore (a) the manhole exchange discharge time series, (b) pipe pressure head, and (c) surface depth profiles around the manhole.
- Scale for SFINCS: the manhole is 0.24 m across on an 8.2 × 4 m plane. Resolving the surcharge plume needs a grid of roughly 1–5 cm (about 160×80 to 820×400 cells). That is cheap computationally, but it pushes SFINCS (designed for m-scale cells and local-inertial physics) far outside its usual range. Martins et al. (2017) show that even full SWE solvers need calibration when surcharge dominates surface inflow, so a SFINCS run should be judged mainly on exchanged volumes and far-field depths, not near-field jet structure.

## 2. Other laboratory facilities (Coimbra, A Coruña, Kyoto, China, Korea, Barcelona, Liège) and full-scale/real-event datasets for dual-drainage validation

### Takeaway
The strongest open alternative to Sheffield, and the best suited to SFINCS–SWMM, is the **University of A Coruña (CITEEC) large-scale facility**. It is about 100 m² with roofs, streets, inlets, manholes and sewers, plus a rainfall simulator and inflow generators. Its dataset (Sañudo et al., 2024, *Hydrological Processes*) is on Zenodo and was used to validate the Iber–SWMM coupling, the closest existing analogue to SFINCS–SWMM. The 36 m² WASHTREET street (Naves et al., 2020, *Scientific Data*) is also openly available. Coimbra (DD-MLE), Kyoto (Ujigawa) and Wuhan/China facilities give component-scale or street-scale exchange data, but I found no public data deposits for them. For real-world data, the Swiss UWO dataset (ESSD 2026) is an open, high-resolution sewer-monitoring dataset that includes a validated hydrodynamic model, but it has no surface-flood observations.

## 3. UK Environment Agency 2D benchmarking (Néelz & Pender 2010, 2013): Test 8A / 8B content and 1D–2D sewer elements

### Takeaway
The EA suite has two urban tests. **Test 8A**, "Rainfall and point source surface flow in urban areas", is surface-only: uniform rainfall plus a point-source inflow representing surcharge. **Test 8B**, "Urban surface flow from a surcharging sewer", adds a **1D pipe (culvert) and manhole network** linked to the 2D surface. Its inflow hydrograph enters at the upstream end of a culvert, and manhole discharge is an output. Test 8B is the only 1D–2D sewer benchmark in the EA programme. It is a model-intercomparison test with no measured truth. The 2013 report (SC120002) supersedes the 2010 report, and the benchmark model data are downloadable from GOV.UK as a ZIP.

### Cited Findings
- **Néelz S, Pender G (2010).** *Benchmarking of 2D Hydraulic Modelling Packages.* Environment Agency Science Report **SC080035/SR2**. The report's list of tests includes **Test 8A "Rainfall and point source surface flow in urban areas"**, intended to assess whether packages simulate shallow urban flows with rainfall inputs, and **Test 8B "Surface flow from a surcharging sewer in urban areas"**. **Confidence: High** — [EA report PDF (gov.uk assets)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a75765c40f0b6360e4744dd/scho0510bsno-e-e.pdf)
- **Néelz S, Pender G (2013).** *Benchmarking the latest generation of 2D hydraulic modelling packages.* Environment Agency report **SC120002**, August 2013, with Heriot-Watt University; the project ran April 2012 – August 2013. SC120002 consolidates all package simulations into one volume and **supersedes the 2010 report (now withdrawn)**. The GOV.UK publication page lists the technical report, summaries, a desktop review and a **"Benchmarking model data (38.7 MB) ZIP"**. **Confidence: High** — [GOV.UK publication page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/benchmarking-the-latest-generation-of-2d-hydraulic-flood-modelling-packages); [GOV.UK FCERM research page](https://gov.uk/flood-and-coastal-erosion-risk-management-research-reports/2d-benchmarking-evaluating-the-latest-generation-of-the-hydraulic-models-for-fcrm); [EA consultation copy of report](https://consult.environment-agency.gov.uk/engagement/1a33644d/results/appendix-6---neelz--s.---pender--g.--2013--benchmarking-the-latest-generation-of-2d-hydraulic-modelling-packages.-bristol_environment-agency.pdf)
- **Test 8B content.** It includes **1D pipe network elements** representing underground flow, while 8A uses the same surface source without the pipe network. The report figures cover the Test 8B DEM, inflow and output locations, an **inflow hydrograph at the upstream end of a culvert**, and **manhole discharge**. TUFLOW GPU could not run 8B because it "includes 1D pipe and manhole features"; TUFLOW FV / HPC reports from 2017/2019 include 8B results. **Confidence: Medium-High** (vendor-hosted reproductions of the EA tests) — [TUFLOW 2012 submission](https://tuflow.com/media/5004/2012-uk-ea-benchmarking-of-2d-hydraulic-modelling-packages-tuflow-submission.pdf); [TUFLOW 2017 update](https://www.tuflow.com/media/5017/2017-uk-ea-2d-benchmarking-results-update-latest-tuflow-solvers.pdf); [TUFLOW 2019 update](https://www.tuflow.com/media/5019/2019-uk-ea-2d-benchmarking-results-update-latest-tuflow-solvers.pdf)
- **Test 8A domain size.** A 2026 arXiv paper that adapts Test 8A describes a 0.4 km × 0.96 km urban area on a 2 m grid (201 × 483 cells), noting that its setting differs from the original. **Confidence: Medium-Low** for original geometry — [arXiv 2605.02959](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.02959)
- **Used in published 1D–2D SWMM-coupled validation: Fernández-Pato J, García-Navarro P (2021).** "An efficient GPU implementation of a coupled overland-sewer hydraulic model with pollutant transport." *Hydrology* 8(4), 146. DOI: 10.3390/hydrology8040146. RiverFlow2D-GPU was fully coupled with **EPA SWMM** (RiverFlow2D-GPU UD). Case 1 was **UK EA Case 8b**, which showed good agreement with the other benchmark models. Case 2 was a "Storm Drainage Capacity Test" and Case 3 pollutant transport. A speed-up of more than 100× is reported. This is a direct precedent for a 2D–SWMM coupling validated on EA 8B. **Confidence: High** — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2306-5338/8/4/146/htm); [Zaguán (Unizar) record](https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/110569)
- A 2023 *MethodsX* paper, "A rapid flood inundation model for urban flood analyses", also used the "EAT8B" case. **Confidence: Medium** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2215016123002017)
- Vendor claim: AquaTwin Sewer 2D reports passing Tests 8A and 8B against 21 reference models. This is not independent. **Confidence: Low** — [Aquanuity](https://aquanuity.com/resources/validation-of-aquatwin-sewer-2d-against-the-uk-environment-agency-2d-hydraulic-model-benchmark-suite/)
- A Zenodo record titled **"Benchmarking of 2D Hydraulic Modelling Packages: files"** (15256842) appeared in search results as holding the files to reproduce the tests. Uploader, licence and contents are unverified. **Confidence: Low-Medium** — [Zenodo 15256842](https://zenodo.org/records/15256842)
- **SFINCS and the EA suite.** I found no published SFINCS validation against the EA urban tests. The newest SFINCS validation found is for riverine floods: Sadana et al., *HESS* 30, 5491 (2026), against satellite flood extents for 499 historical events. The SFINCS reference paper is Leijnse T, van Ormondt M, Nederhoff K, van Dongeren A (2021), "Modeling compound flooding in coastal systems using a computationally efficient reduced-physics solver: including fluvial, pluvial, tidal, wind- and wave-driven processes", *Coastal Engineering* (DOI 10.1016/j.coastaleng.2020.103796, unverified, from memory). **Confidence: High** for Sadana; Medium for Leijnse DOI — [HESS 30/5491/2026](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/30/5491/2026/)

### Inferences
- Test 8B is the natural "industry-standard" 1D–2D check for SFINCS–SWMM. It runs at metre scale (2 m-class grid), which is SFINCS's native range, it has real urban topography, and it allows direct comparison with many commercial and academic packages (TUFLOW, InfoWorks ICM, MIKE, RiverFlow2D-UD, etc.). Because it has no measured truth, it shows consistency with other models, not physical accuracy. It should be paired with a laboratory dataset (Sheffield or A Coruña).
- Running Test 8A (no pipe) and 8B (with pipe) gives a clean way to isolate the effect of the SWMM coupling, since the same surface source is used with and without the pipe network.
- A SFINCS-only run of Tests 1–8A, with no published precedent found, would be a useful baseline before testing the coupling.

### Gaps
- Exact Test 8A/8B specifications (rainfall intensity and duration, inflow hydrograph peak, culvert/pipe diameter and length, manhole location, simulation duration, required resolution, and the list of packages that ran 8B in 2013) could not be retrieved. The gov.uk PDF and ZIP were blocked, so the report sections must be read directly. Whether the 8A/8B domain is in Glasgow, like the Hunter et al. (2008) catchment, is **unverified**.
- I found no evidence of any other sewer/1D–2D benchmark in the EA programme beyond Test 8B.

## 4. Analytical and idealised verification tests for coupled sewer–surface schemes

### Takeaway
There is no widely adopted analytical benchmark specifically for coupled sewer–surface models comparable to the EA suite. Verification in the literature relies on (a) idealised test cases built by model developers, such as junction/pipe tests, dam-break interacting with surcharging manholes, simplified streets and "storm drainage capacity" tests, (b) EA Test 8B, and (c) comparisons with 3D CFD. Mass-conservation (volume-balance) checks across the 1D–2D interface are standard practice but rarely published as a stand-alone benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- **Li Q, Liang Q, Xia X (2020).** "A novel 1D-2D coupled model for hydrodynamic simulation of flows in drainage networks." *Advances in Water Resources* 137, 103519 (DOI not seen; unverified). The model was validated against **one experiment and several idealised test cases**. Its junction treatment is said to conserve mass and momentum. The open PDF is in the Loughborough repository. **Confidence: Medium-High** — [Loughborough repository PDF](https://repository.lboro.ac.uk/articles/A_novel_1D-2D_coupled_model_for_hydrodynamic_simulation_of_flows_in_drainage_networks/11816340/files/21601008.pdf)
- **Moodi et al. (2024)**, *JFRM* 17(1), e12953. Idealised cases were checked against STAR-CD 3D Navier–Stokes, including **dam-break flow on a surface interacting with two surcharging manholes**, and then against unsteady-surcharge experiments. **Confidence: High** — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jfr3.12953)
- **Fernández-Pato & García-Navarro (2021)**, *Hydrology* 8(4), 146. Uses EA Case 8b plus a **"Storm Drainage Capacity Test"** for a GPU 2D–SWMM coupling. **Confidence: High** — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2306-5338/8/4/146/htm)
- **Sañudo, Cea, Puertas (2020)**, *Water* 12, 2647. Includes a **simplified urban street** case as the first verification step of Iber–SWMM. **Confidence: High** — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/12/9/2647)
- arXiv 1402.4254, "Flooding in urban drainage systems: coupling hyperbolic conservation laws for sewer systems and surface flow". Manhole models use a separate ODE derived from mass and momentum conservation, and idealised coupled test cases are shown. The journal version (likely Borsche & Klar, *Int. J. Numer. Methods Fluids*, 2014) is unverified. **Confidence: Medium** — [arXiv 1402.4254](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.4254)
- **Kesserwani et al. (2015)** and **Martins et al. (2017)** treat the surcharging manhole as a **source/sink** in the 2D mass equation. This is the same coupling form SFINCS would use for SWMM node overflow/inflow. The single-manhole surcharge on a flat plane therefore serves as the de facto idealised test, with Sheffield experimental data as reference. **Confidence: Medium-High** — [Sheffield PDF (UDM 2015)](https://sheffield.ac.uk/media/7328/download); [UWJ 2017](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1573062X.2017.1279193)
- An open GitHub repository, **"benchmark1d2d"** (scott-jeffers), describes itself as "open V&V benchmarks for 1D/2D/coupled hydraulic solvers (physics + SWMM/EPANET parity)". It could not be inspected (access blocked), and its provenance and peer-review status are unknown. **Confidence: Low** — [GitHub](https://github.com/scott-jeffers/benchmark1d2d)

### Inferences
- A defensible verification ladder for SFINCS–SWMM, assembled from the precedents above (my synthesis, not a published standard):
  1. **Volume-balance test.** Closed domain, a single node surcharging onto a flat or tilted plane and later draining back. Check that the cumulative SWMM node overflow equals the change in SFINCS volume to machine or tolerance precision, following the source/sink coupling used by Kesserwani/Martins.
  2. **Single surcharging manhole.** Compare with Sheffield data (exchange discharge, depth around the manhole).
  3. **Surface→sewer drainage through an inlet.** Sheffield circular-inlet data (JHydroinf 2018) or the Coimbra gully data.
  4. **Street / small district with rainfall.** A Coruña ~100 m² facility, and Iber–SWMM results as a model-to-model reference.
  5. **Urban-scale intercomparison.** EA Test 8A vs. 8B.
  6. **Real-event case**, if municipal data are available.
- The SWMM side alone can be checked against SWMM's own results with the surface disabled ("parity"). This mirrors the "SWMM parity" idea in the benchmark1d2d repo, but no peer-reviewed source formalises it.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed **analytical** (closed-form) solution for coupled pipe-surcharge + 2D surface flow was found. Tilted-plane/V-catchment overland-flow analytical tests exist for surface-only models, but no source tying them to sewer coupling was retrieved in this session.
- The contents of Li et al. (2020) idealised tests and of the "Storm Drainage Capacity Test" (Fernández-Pato 2021) were not read in full.
- The benchmark1d2d repository could not be inspected.

### Inferences
- **SFINCS fit**:
  - EA 8A/8B is a natural fit: metre-scale regular grid and rainfall plus point source.
  - The A Coruña facility is a good fit: decimetre/centimetre grid over about 100 m² gives roughly 10⁴–10⁵ cells, which is trivial cost, and it is rainfall-driven with drainage nodes. Roof-to-street connections may need SWMM subcatchments or SFINCS source points.
  - Sheffield is feasible but demanding: cm-scale grid, and a surcharge jet that a local-inertial solver will not resolve locally, so it should be evaluated on integral exchange volumes and depths away from the manhole.
  - Coimbra/Kyoto component-scale rigs mainly test the exchange formula. They are useful for calibrating SFINCS–SWMM weir/orifice coefficients, not 2D surface dynamics.
- Since Martins et al. (2017, JHydroinf) showed that a local-inertial surface model coupled to a 1D sewer gives exchange volumes close to full SWE on a real case, SFINCS–SWMM can reasonably be expected to perform similarly at urban scale. This must be demonstrated, not assumed.
- Using the same datasets as Iber–SWMM (A Coruña) and RiverFlow2D-GPU UD (EA 8B), both SWMM-coupled, would let the paper benchmark SFINCS–SWMM against published SWMM-based couplings.

### Gaps
- Licences and file formats of all Zenodo deposits are unverified (Zenodo was blocked).
- I could not confirm whether any published study applied SFINCS at laboratory (cm) scale. SFINCS's minimum practical cell size, Manning-n handling at lab scale and time-step limits for such grids were not researched here.
- Exact EA Test 8B parameters remain to be read from the SC120002 report or data ZIP.
