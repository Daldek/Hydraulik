## Q1. Is there any prior work (paper, preprint, thesis, abstract, repo, report) that couples SFINCS with SWMM / pyswmm / another sewer model, or uses SFINCS and SWMM together in one study?

### Takeaway
No direct SFINCS–SWMM (or SFINCS–any pipe network) coupling, online or offline, was found in any searched source as of 2026-10-20. The nearest thing is a USGS repository that couples SFINCS to MODFLOW 6 through the SFINCS BMI/XMI (xmipy), and in the same project couples SWMM to MODFLOW 6 and D-Flow FM. SWMM never exchanges water with SFINCS there, but the same tool chain is already in place, so this is the most dangerous near-miss.

### Inferences
- A claim of "first two-way coupling of SFINCS with SWMM" looks defensible in the narrow sense as of 2026-10-20, but only as "to our knowledge". The USGS LISS project (Hughes/Prigiobbe/Masterson) already drives SFINCS through xmipy and SWMM through pyswmm inside one coupling framework. A SFINCS–SWMM link could appear there, or in the forthcoming Hughes et al. manuscript, at any time, and a reviewer from that community would know this.
- The phrase "first use of SFINCS and SWMM in one study" is riskier than "first two-way coupling". It cannot be excluded that consultancy or Deltares project reports (not indexed) used SWMM results as SFINCS point sources (offline/one-way). Nothing like that was found.
- "Via BMI" is not novel by itself. SFINCS has been coupled through its XMI/BMI (xmipy) to MODFLOW 6 in public code (USGS repo above). Only the SFINCS–SWMM pairing through BMI is new.

## Q2. What are the closest analogues: fast/reduced-complexity 2D models (local inertial, CA, subgrid, GPU) coupled with SWMM or other 1D sewer models, and BMI-based flood–sewer couplings?

### Takeaway
"Dual drainage with a fast/reduced-physics 2D model + SWMM" is well established. LISFLOOD-FP (local inertial, the same equation family as SFINCS) has been coupled to SWMM since at least 2017, and an open-source two-way LISFLOOD-FP 8.1–SWMM coupler appeared on GitHub on 14 October 2026. The local-inertial GPU solver Inunda (arXiv, July 2026) couples bidirectionally with SWMM and reportedly uses exchange limiters and damping. Itzï, CA models (WCA2D/CADDIES) and 3Di (subgrid + 1D sewer) are further precedents. A SWMM BMI wrapper already exists (eWaterCycle).

**Local-inertial GPU solver + SWMM (2026), very close in physics and in stabilisation**
- Li, Z. (2026). "Inunda: A GPU-Native, Agent-enabled, Differentiable Solver for High-Resolution Flood Inundation Modeling." arXiv:2607.09614 (July 2026; University of Colorado Boulder per search summary). ### Inferences
- A reviewer can cite at least four precedents for "reduced-physics or fast 2D + SWMM dual drainage": LISFLOOD-FP ACC + SWMM (2017– and the October 2026 open-source coupler), Itzï + SWMM (2017–), CA/WCA2D/CADDIES + SWMM (2020–2022), and Inunda local-inertial + SWMM (July 2026). 3Di adds "subgrid fast 2D + 1D sewer". Any claim that the concept of fast/reduced-complexity dual drainage is new would be rejected.
- The FloodGroup LISFLOOD-FP–SWMM coupler is the most dangerous analogue for "first local-inertial + SWMM open coupling". It was released one week before this search (2026-10-14), uses the same local-inertial physics as SFINCS, is open source, and reportedly uses supply-limited (volume-capped) withdrawals and exact-time-landing exchange intervals. The methods paper should cite it, note that it has no peer-reviewed description yet, and set itself apart by model (SFINCS, with subgrid and quadtree), coupling mechanism (BMI/XMI from Python instead of a monolithic compiled executable) and application (compound coastal flooding).

A "volume-based exchange robust to adaptive time step" is therefore at most incremental, unless it is shown specifically for SFINCS's adaptive CFL step plus subgrid volume bookkeeping under `update_until`-style BMI stepping, with a documented mass-balance error.
- SWMM already has a BMI wrapper (eWaterCycle) and SFINCS already has an XMI/BMI used in coupling (USGS). Coupling these two BMI-exposed models is engineering-new, not concept-new.

## Q3. Do compound-flood frameworks that include SFINCS (e.g. FloodAdapt, US coastal city studies) also include sewer/drainage networks?

### Takeaway
No SFINCS-based compound-flood framework or study was found that includes a pipe/sewer network. SFINCS handles drainage with point/line structures (pumps, culverts, check valves) or not at all, and SFINCS-based global and urban studies acknowledge that drainage is missing or underrepresented. Coastal compound flooding with explicit sewer–surface coupling does exist, but with other 2D engines (TRITON-SWMM in Norfolk; an Earth's Future 2026 Hoboken study with an unidentified 1D/2D model) or with groundwater–sewer couplings (USGS MODFLOW–SWMM, Long Island).

## Q4. Overall: what precisely would be novel, and what is NOT novel?

### Takeaway
Defensible: "to our knowledge, the first (published, open) two-way coupling of SFINCS with EPA SWMM for dual-drainage simulation, implemented through SFINCS's BMI/XMI interface and pyswmm". NOT defensible: novelty of reduced-complexity/local-inertial dual drainage as such, of BMI-based coupling of SFINCS, of SWMM exposed through BMI, of volume-limited exchange, or of exchange stabilisation by damping or limiting. Methodological contributions must be framed narrowly and tied to SFINCS specifics (adaptive CFL step, subgrid volume–level relation, quadtree grid, compound coastal boundary at outfalls).

### Inferences
**Claim-by-claim verdict (for the methods paper):**
1. "First coupling of SFINCS with SWMM": defensible with hedging ("to our knowledge, the first two-way coupling of SFINCS and EPA SWMM"). Avoid "first use of SFINCS and SWMM together", since offline/one-way use in unpublished consultancy work cannot be excluded. Mention the USGS LISS framework (SFINCS↔MODFLOW 6 and SWMM↔MODFLOW 6/D-Flow FM) as the closest related coupling.
2. "Dual drainage via BMI": the BMI part is not novel by itself (SFINCS XMI/BMI with xmipy: USGS; SWMM BMI: eWaterCycle; DLL-based generic SWMM linking: Leandro & Martins 2016). It is novel only in combination ("first SFINCS–SWMM dual drainage implemented through the SFINCS BMI/XMI and pyswmm, without modifying either source code"). Note that SFINCS's BMI is XMI-flavoured and not BMI 2.0-compliant (SFINCS docs), which is a practical caveat worth reporting.
3. "Reduced-physics/fast 2D dual drainage": NOT novel (LISFLOOD-FP+SWMM since 2017, Itzï+SWMM, WCA2D/CADDIES+SWMM, 3Di, Inunda, GPU couplings).
4. "Volume-based exchange robust to adaptive time step": partially anticipated. LISFLOOD-SWMM uses supply-limited withdrawals, exact-time-landing intervals and volume continuity at outfalls; Inunda uses a hard volume limiter. Novelty must be narrowed to the SFINCS context. For example: exchange defined as volumes accumulated over SFINCS's variable internal steps between BMI `update_until` calls; consistency with the SFINCS subgrid volume–level tables; and a demonstrated closed mass balance across both models with quantified error vs coupling interval.
5. "Documented instability with EXTRAN (SWMM dynamic wave) and its stabilisation": coupling instabilities and their damping/limiting are known in general. A systematic, reproducible documentation of the failure mode specific to SWMM5 DYNWAVE surcharge plus a reduced-physics adaptive-step 2D model, with a benchmark showing which remedy works, is a legitimate incremental contribution, not a conceptual first. It should be framed as "we document and resolve", not "we discover".
6. Potentially distinctive angle: compound coastal dual drainage with a single reduced-physics model, where SFINCS supplies both surface flooding and dynamic outfall tailwater from surge/tide for SWMM, at SFINCS's speed. The mechanism is studied elsewhere (TRITON-SWMM Norfolk; Xi et al. 2026 Hoboken), but apparently not with a reduced-physics compound model such as SFINCS.
7. Timing risk: two very close works appeared within the last ~3 months (Inunda, July 2026; LISFLOOD-SWMM coupler, 14 Oct 2026), and a USGS group is actively coupling SFINCS and SWMM side by side. The window for a "first SFINCS–SWMM" claim may be short; a preprint or Zenodo DOI soon would help establish priority.
